# Current Electricity — revision notes

## 1. Current and drift
- Electric current I = Q / t. Unit: ampere (A) = 1 coulomb per second.
- Electrons drift slowly (about 1 mm/s) opposite to the field. The bulb lights instantly because the electric field travels through the wire almost at the speed of light, pushing all electrons at once.
- I = n e A v_d, where n = number of free electrons per m³, e = electron charge, A = cross-section area, v_d = drift speed.

## 2. Ohm's law
- At constant temperature, V = I R for a metallic conductor.
- A material that follows this (straight V–I graph) is **ohmic**. A diode and a filament bulb are **non-ohmic**.
- A filament bulb's V–I graph bends because the filament heats up and its resistance rises.

## 3. Resistance and resistivity
- R = ρ L / A. ρ (rho) is resistivity — a property of the material, not of the wire's shape.
- Double the length → double the resistance. Double the area → half the resistance.
- Stretching a wire to twice its length (same volume) makes the area half, so R becomes **4 times**.
- For metals, resistivity increases with temperature. For semiconductors, it decreases.

## 4. Series and parallel
- Series: R = R1 + R2 + …  Same current through each.
- Parallel: 1/R = 1/R1 + 1/R2 + …  Same voltage across each. The total is always smaller than the smallest resistor.
- Example: 6 Ω and 3 Ω in parallel → 1/R = 1/6 + 1/3 = 1/2, so R = 2 Ω.

## 5. Power and energy
- P = V I = I²R = V²/R.
- Household appliances are connected in **parallel**, so each gets the full 230 V and can be switched on or off on its own.
- Electricity bills use kWh: 1 kWh = 1000 W for 1 hour = 3.6 × 10⁶ J.
- Example: a 2000 W heater for 3 hours uses 6 kWh.

## 6. Kirchhoff's laws
- **Junction rule (KCL):** total current into a junction = total current out. (Conservation of charge.)
- **Loop rule (KVL):** sum of potential differences around any closed loop = 0. (Conservation of energy.)

## 7. EMF and internal resistance
- EMF (ε) is the energy given per coulomb by the cell. Terminal voltage V = ε − I r, where r is internal resistance.
- When no current flows, V = ε. When a large current flows, V drops — that's why car headlights dim when the starter motor runs.
